Risk Atlas · clot

How a clot can form at a rupture site and reduce blood flow.

This risk atlas picks up where the Rupture atlas stops: exposed plaque contents are already in contact with flowing blood. It shows how platelets, clotting proteins, and a growing thrombus can turn a surface injury into a blood-flow problem. Use the slider to move from exposure to platelet attachment, fibrin mesh formation, clot growth, and severe flow reduction.

What this atlas does

  • Shows clot formation as a local process at a rupture site.
  • Separates platelet attachment, fibrin mesh formation, clot growth, and flow reduction.
  • Stops at reduced or blocked blood flow without turning this page into heart attack or stroke.
  • Supports better questions and discussions with your health team.

What this means

This page begins after the rupture step. The question here is how blood reacts at the exposed surface.

What this does not show

This module does not show your personal clotting risk, diagnose a clot, or predict an event. It explains a simplified local process.

What clot formation can involve

  • Exposed plaque contents after a surface break
  • Platelet attachment and activation
  • A fibrin mesh that helps stabilise the clot
  • Trapped blood elements and progressive flow reduction

Discuss with your health team

Ask how your wider risk context is being considered, especially plaque burden, Lp(a), ApoB, inflammation markers, blood pressure, metabolic risk, medicines, symptoms, and family history.

This visualisation is educational. It does not diagnose, predict, or treat any condition. It is not a substitute for advice from your health team. If symptoms suggest an emergency, seek urgent medical help.
