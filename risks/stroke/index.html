When brain blood flow is threatened

This risk atlas picks up where Clot and Cascade leave off. It shows one possible stroke pathway: a clot or blockage reducing blood flow to part of the brain. Use the slider to move from brain-supply context to flow interruption, oxygen shortfall, warning signs, and emergency evaluation.

Scope of this atlas

  • Focuses on a blockage-type stroke pathway, often called ischaemic stroke.
  • Shows blood-flow interruption and downstream brain-territory stress.
  • Connects sudden warning signs to urgent emergency evaluation.
  • Does not cover bleeding stroke in detail; that is a different emergency pathway.
  • Does not tell you whether symptoms are safe, temporary, or harmless.

Stage explanation

Brain supply context

The page begins with a brain artery supplying a downstream brain territory.

What this means

A blockage-type stroke pathway matters because brain tissue depends on continuous blood flow and oxygen supply.

What this does not show

This is not your anatomy, not a symptom checker, and not a diagnosis. It does not show whether you are having a stroke.

What stroke can involve

  • A brain-supplying artery and the territory it supports
  • A clot, embolus (a travelling clot), or blockage that reduces blood flow
  • Oxygen shortfall in downstream brain tissue
  • Sudden warning signs that require emergency evaluation
  • A different pathway for bleeding stroke, which this page does not model in detail

Discuss with your health team

Ask how your stroke risk is being interpreted in the context of blood pressure, rhythm concerns such as atrial fibrillation, Lp(a), ApoB, diabetes or insulin resistance, smoking exposure, medicines, symptoms, previous TIA or stroke, and family history.

This visualisation is educational. If symptoms suggest stroke or another emergency, seek urgent medical help immediately. Do not use this page to decide whether symptoms are safe.
