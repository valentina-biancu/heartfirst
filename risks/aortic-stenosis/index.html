Risk Atlas · Aortic stenosis

When a narrowed valve makes the heart work harder

This risk atlas picks up where the Valve Calcification atlas stops. It shows how a narrowed aortic valve can restrict blood flow out of the heart, increase pressure load on the left ventricle, and create a pathway toward monitoring, symptom review, and specialist valve decisions.

Scope of this atlas

  • Focuses on narrowing of the aortic valve opening and the pressure load this can create.
  • Shows simplified valve, flow, and heart-strain cues, not an echocardiogram or CT scan.
  • Separates aortic stenosis from coronary artery blockage, heart attack, and stroke.
  • Stops at health team evaluation and valve-decision context, not personal diagnosis or treatment advice.

Valve pathway

What aortic stenosis can involve

  • A narrowed aortic valve opening.
  • Restricted or reduced blood flow from the heart to the aorta.
  • Higher pressure load on the left ventricle.
  • Symptoms or reduced capacity that need health team review.
  • Monitoring and valve-treatment decisions based on severity, symptoms, and imaging.
Key point: aortic stenosis is not the same as heart attack. One is a valve outflow problem; the other is usually a coronary blood-flow emergency.

Aortic stenosis can sit at the intersection of valve anatomy, age, Lp(a), kidney health, blood pressure, symptoms, and imaging results. If you have valve findings, a murmur, a bicuspid-valve history, echo measurements, or family questions, the task is not to interpret one number alone. It is to organise the full context and prepare the right questions for your health team.
