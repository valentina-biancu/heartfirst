Risk Atlas · Valve calcification

When calcium builds on a heart valve

This risk atlas moves from the arterial event pathway into the valve pathway. It shows how calcium can collect on aortic valve leaflets, how leaflet motion can become more restricted, and how this can prepare the hand-off to aortic stenosis without claiming that every valve deposit becomes severe disease.

Scope of this atlas

  • Focuses on calcium building up on the aortic valve leaflets.
  • Shows stiffness and narrowing as a simplified visual pathway, not as a scan or echocardiogram.
  • Separates valve calcification from coronary artery calcium and artery plaque.
  • Stops before the full aortic-stenosis module, where pressure load and symptoms belong.

Valve pathway

What valve calcification can involve

  • Aortic valve leaflets that need to open and close freely.
  • Calcium deposits collecting on or around the leaflets.
  • Stiffness that can reduce leaflet motion.
  • A smaller opening that can make blood leave the heart less easily.
  • A hand-off to aortic stenosis when narrowing and pressure load become the focus.
Key point: valve calcification is not the same as coronary artery calcium. One involves a heart valve; the other involves artery plaque.

Valve calcification can sit outside the usual cholesterol conversation.
