Risk Atlas · plaque

What plaque is, where it builds, and why it matters.

This risk atlas shows how plaque can develop within an artery wall over time. It helps make clear one process rather than trying to explain every cardiovascular pathway at once. Use the slider to move from a healthy artery to early plaque, a growing lipid core, a fibrous cap, and more advanced artery narrowing.

What this atlas does

  • Shows artery structure and plaque buildup in a visual way.
  • Explains core components such as lipid core, foam cells, fibrous cap, and narrowing.
  • Lets you compare early and advanced stages by using the slider, from a healthy artery to more advanced plaque and narrowing.
  • Supports better questions and discussions with your health team.

Healthy artery

What this means

This baseline view helps show where plaque develops: within the artery wall, not floating freely in the bloodstream.

What this does not show

This module does not show every cause of cardiovascular risk or tell you whether a plaque is present in your own body. It shows one educational process in isolation so the underlying structure is easier to understand.

What plaque is made of

  • Lipid-rich material
  • Inflammatory cells
  • Scar-like fibrous tissue
  • Sometimes calcium

Discuss with your health team

If your risk picture feels incomplete, ask what current checks do and do not show, and whether further context is needed.

This visualisation is educational. It does not diagnose, predict, or treat any condition. It is not a substitute for advice from your health team. If symptoms suggest an emergency, seek urgent medical help.
