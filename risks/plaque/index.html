Risk Atlas · plaque

What plaque is, where it builds, and why it matters.

This risk atlas shows how plaque can develop within an artery wall over time. It focuses on one foundational process, then helps connect this process to later atlas modules on inflammation, rupture, clot, and downstream events. Use the slider to move from a healthy artery to early particle entry, a growing lipid core, a fibrous cap, and more advanced artery narrowing.

What this atlas does

  • Shows artery structure and plaque buildup in a visual way.
  • Explains core components such as endothelium, ApoB-containing particle entry, lipid core, foam cells, fibrous cap, calcium, and narrowing.
  • Lets you compare early and advanced stages by using the slider, from a healthy artery to more advanced plaque and narrowing.
  • Supports better questions and discussions with your health team.

What this means

This baseline view shows where plaque develops: within the artery wall, not floating freely in the bloodstream.

What this does not show

This module does not show every cause of cardiovascular risk or tell you whether a plaque is present in your own body. It shows one educational process in isolation so the underlying structure is easier to understand.

What plaque is made of

  • A lipid-rich core carried into the wall by ApoB-containing particles
  • Inflammatory cells, including foam cells
  • Scar-like fibrous tissue forming a cap over the core
  • Sometimes calcium specks as plaque becomes more advanced

Discuss with your health team
