# HeartFirst Clarify Component Decision Pass v0.1

**Purpose:** Settle the smallest complete set of Clarify components capable of delivering a worthwhile result for the buyer, with clear value for the user and the people they love.

**Commercial principle:** Give away the explanation of what heart risk is and why incomplete information matters. Charge for the guided structure, saved effort, usable outputs, and better preparation.

**Product principle:** The buyer is not paying to be taught a curriculum. They are paying to turn scattered information and concern into something they can use.

---

# 1. Recommended product promise

> **Clarify helps you bring together what is known, see what is missing or unclear, prepare a focused health team conversation, and record what happens next for you or someone you love.**

Supporting explanation:

> Use Clarify for yourself, for someone you love, or together. It turns scattered results, family information, concerns, and unanswered questions into four useful records you can take into a health team conversation and continue using afterwards.

Clarify does not diagnose, calculate risk, decide which tests or treatment someone needs, or replace a health professional.

---

# 2. The result the buyer is paying for

At the end of Clarify, the buyer should be able to say:

> **I can see what is known, what is missing or unclear, what needs to be discussed, and what happens next. I can also see whether any of this may matter to someone I love and what they may need to clarify for themselves.**

The paid value is not the number of worksheets. It is the quality and usefulness of the finished records.

---

# 3. Recommended delivery model

## One integrated Clarify workbook

Clarify should be one guided product, not a folder of eight separate workbooks.

The workbook should:

- ask for information once and reuse it;
- allow the user to work for themselves, someone they love, or both;
- produce four clean take-forward records;
- include short explanations only where they are needed;
- allow pausing and returning;
- work as a fillable file and as a printable version;
- provide a blank reusable copy for private personal use;
- include one fictional worked example.

The user should not have to decide which file to open or which worksheet comes next.

## Four take-forward outputs

1. **Heart Risk Audit Record**
2. **Missing-Information Summary**
3. **Health Team Discussion Brief**
4. **Next-Action Record**

A short **People You Love follow-up section** should be generated from information entered throughout the workbook. It is an explicit part of all four outputs, not an isolated bonus worksheet.

---

# 4. The five product components

## Component 1 — Start with the person and the concern

### User-facing heading

> **Who needs clarity now?**

### Problem it solves

People may buy Clarify because they are worried about themselves, a partner, a relative, a friend, or several people. A product that assumes the buyer is always clarifying only their own risk starts in the wrong place.

### What the user does

The user identifies:

- who the immediate concern is about;
- what happened or what raised the question;
- whether there is an upcoming appointment;
- what they most need to leave Clarify knowing or prepared to discuss.

Suggested opening questions:

> Who are you using Clarify for today?

- Me
- Someone I love
- Both of us
- I am not sure yet

> What brought you here?

- A test result
- A diagnosis
- A symptom or concern
- A heart event affecting someone close to me
- Family history
- A routine check that did not answer my questions
- Several pieces of information that do not feel connected
- Something else

> What would make this useful today?

- Knowing what information I already have
- Seeing what may be missing or unclear
- Preparing for a health team conversation
- Helping someone I love prepare
- Recording what should happen next

### Output

**Clarify Starting Point**

A brief statement naming:

- the person whose information is being organised;
- the immediate concern;
- the next conversation or decision the user is preparing for.

### People-you-love requirement

This component makes loved ones visible from the first question. It does not assume that biological family is the most important relationship.

Where the user is helping another adult, the workbook should remind them to involve that person and respect their privacy and choices.

### Design target

First useful result within approximately five minutes.

## Component 2 — Bring the facts together

### User-facing heading

> **What do we know?**

### Formal output

**Heart Risk Audit Record**

### Problem it solves

Relevant information is often spread across laboratory reports, patient portals, letters, memories, family conversations, appointment notes, and different people.

The user should not have to invent a structure or know in advance what belongs in it.

### What the user records

Only factual information that is known or reported as uncertain:

- results, units, dates, and source;
- diagnoses and previous heart or vascular events;
- medicines and relevant treatment history;
- symptoms or concerns to raise with a health professional;
- blood pressure, glucose, cholesterol, and other available measurements;
- imaging, procedures, and referrals;
- relevant family health information;
- lifestyle, work, household, and environmental context;
- previous advice, decisions, and unfinished follow-up.

Every entry should allow:

- **Known**
- **Not sure**
- **Needs confirming**
- **Not available**

The workbook should not ask the user to interpret a result.

### People-you-love requirement

For every relevant person, the tool distinguishes:

- **Possible inherited relevance:** a biological relative may share an inherited vulnerability.
- **Shared conditions:** people may share a home, routines, smoking exposure, food environment, stress, work, pollution, or barriers to care.
- **Their own separate concern:** a partner, friend, or relative may have an independent risk, result, diagnosis, or symptom.
- **Support:** someone may help with information, appointments, decisions, or follow-through.

These ideas should be written as plain questions, not presented as technical categories.

Examples:

> Could a biological relative share an inherited risk?

> Do you and this person share a home, routines, or an environment that may affect heart health?

> Are you concerned about something that belongs to their health rather than yours?

> Are they helping you, or are you helping them, with records, appointments, or follow-through?

### Output standard

The record must be short enough to use. It should not become a complete medical history.

A one-page summary should show:

- the main known facts;
- dates and sources;
- whose information each item concerns;
- what needs checking.

---

## Component 3 — Find what is missing or unclear

### User-facing heading

> **What still needs to be clarified?**

### Formal output

**Missing-Information Summary**

### Problem it solves

People often know that something feels incomplete but cannot name the gap. They may also mistake an absent result for a result that is normal, or a second-hand family account for confirmed information.

### What the user does

The workbook helps identify information that is:

- missing;
- outdated;
- reported without units or dates;
- remembered but not confirmed;
- held by another clinic or person;
- unclear in meaning;
- not yet discussed;
- relevant to someone else rather than the user;
- important but not currently obtainable.

The tool should not produce a list of tests the person must obtain.

It should help the user state the uncertainty clearly.

Examples:

> I have the result, but not the units.

> I was told a relative had a heart problem, but I do not know the diagnosis or age.

> My partner and I share several risks at home, but we have not discussed what we could change together.

> I do not know whether this result affects biological relatives.

> I do not know who should answer this question.

### Prioritisation

Each gap is marked:

- **Needs attention now**
- **Useful to clarify**
- **Can wait**
- **Not possible to clarify at present**

This is the user’s organisational priority, not a clinical urgency score.

### People-you-love requirement

The summary must identify:

- information that may matter to a biological relative;
- shared conditions that may affect several people;
- a loved one’s separate unanswered concern;
- information another person may be able to confirm;
- whether someone may need their own Clarify record or health team conversation.

### Output standard

The summary should fit on one page and answer:

> What is missing or unclear, why does it matter to the conversation, and who does it concern?

---

## Component 4 — Prepare the conversation

### User-facing heading

> **What needs to be discussed?**

### Formal output

**Health Team Discussion Brief**

### Problem it solves

Appointments are short. People may forget their questions, provide too much background, or leave without addressing the concern that brought them there.

### What the user does

The tool converts the audit and missing-information summary into a focused brief.

The user selects:

- the purpose of the conversation;
- the three to five questions that matter most;
- the facts the health team needs to see;
- records or results to bring;
- what remains uncertain;
- what decision, explanation, or follow-up they hope to leave with;
- practical barriers that may affect the next step.

Plain question prompts:

> What do you most need help understanding?

> What information might change what happens next?

> Is there anything important that has not been discussed before?

> Does this raise a question for a biological relative or someone who shares your home or routines?

> Is someone you love preparing for their own conversation?

> What would a useful outcome from this appointment look like?

### People-you-love requirement

The brief can be prepared:

- for the user’s appointment;
- with a loved one for their own appointment;
- for a shared household concern;
- to ask whether inherited information should be communicated to relatives.

It must keep each person’s information separate and must not ask one person’s clinician to assess another adult who is not their patient.

### Output standard

The finished brief should be:

- one or two pages;
- readable in less than two minutes;
- clearly divided into facts, questions, and desired decisions;
- usable on a phone, on paper, or as a saved file.

---

## Component 5 — Record what happens next

### User-facing heading

> **What happens next?**

### Formal output

**Next-Action Record**

### Problem it solves

People often leave an appointment with several statements but no reliable record of what was agreed, who will do it, or when it should happen.

### What the user records

- what was explained;
- what was agreed;
- who owns each next action;
- what information, appointment, test, referral, or follow-up is expected;
- when it should happen;
- what remains unresolved;
- when the issue should be reviewed;
- whether the action concerns the user, someone they love, or both.

Plain prompts:

> What is the next action?

> Who is responsible for it?

> What should happen before the next conversation?

> When will you check whether it happened?

> What is still unanswered?

> Does someone you love need information, their own conversation, or support with a shared change?

### People-you-love requirement

The final section should produce a concise **People You Love follow-up note** containing only relevant actions:

- information to share;
- a person who may need their own clarification or health team conversation;
- a shared household issue to discuss;
- support the user has asked for;
- support another person has requested.

The tool must make clear:

- the user does not have to involve anyone;
- information should not be shared where it is unsafe or inappropriate;
- another adult remains responsible for their own decisions;
- a possible inherited relevance is not a diagnosis.

### Output standard

The record should fit on one page and remain useful after the appointment.

It is the point where Clarify hands into Navigate or Prevent when more work is needed, but the record is complete and useful on its own.

# 5. What the buyer should receive

## Core paid package

1. **Clarify integrated workbook**
2. **Four clean take-forward output pages**
3. **People You Love prompts and follow-up note throughout**
4. **One fictional worked example**
5. **A blank reusable copy for private personal use**
6. **Short instructions for saving, printing, and taking the records to an appointment**

## Optional support, not core work

- glossary;
- evidence and sources;
- emergency guidance;
- explanation of what standard tests do and do not show;
- short examples of useful questions;
- accessibility and privacy guidance.

These should be available when needed without forcing the buyer to read them before starting.

# 7. Time and effort targets

These are design targets to test with real users, not public promises yet.

- **First useful result:** within 5 minutes
- **Heart Risk Audit first pass:** 15–20 minutes when records are available
- **Missing-Information Summary:** 5–10 minutes
- **Discussion Brief:** 5–10 minutes
- **Next-Action Record:** completed during or after the health team conversation
- **Typical preparation before an appointment:** approximately 25–40 minutes
- **Pause and resume:** essential
- **No compulsory reading before starting:** essential

A person with extensive records or several people to consider may need longer. The product should encourage one person or one immediate concern at a time rather than forcing the user to document an entire household in one sitting.

---

# 8. Experience standard

The product should feel like a calm, capable person helping the user prepare.

Every page should:

- explain why the question is being asked;
- use ordinary words;
- ask only for information that has a clear use;
- show what the answer will contribute to;
- allow “I do not know” without making the user feel they have failed;
- distinguish fact, uncertainty, concern, and decision;
- avoid blame;
- avoid assuming close or safe family relationships;
- make room for a partner, friend, carer, or chosen family;
- preserve privacy and separate ownership of care;
- end with something the user can use.

Avoid:

- “unlock”;
- “empower”;
- “transform”;
- “complete risk picture”;
- “actionable insights”;
- “connect the dots” where no concrete connection is being made;
- “protect your family” as a universal duty;
- medical-sounding scores or colour-coded threat levels;
- long educational passages before the first task;
- questions that exist only because they are interesting.

---

# 9. Product completion test

Clarify is complete when the user has:

1. named the person and concern they are clarifying;
2. brought the main known facts into one usable record;
3. identified the important missing or unclear information;
4. prepared a focused health team discussion brief;
5. recorded the next action and unresolved questions;
6. considered what may matter to the people they love, including inherited, shared, separate, and support needs;
7. left with records that can be used without buying Navigate or Prevent.

---

# 10. Recommended decision

## Lock as the Clarify core

1. **Who needs clarity now?** — Clarify Starting Point
2. **What do we know?** — Heart Risk Audit Record
3. **What still needs to be clarified?** — Missing-Information Summary
4. **What needs to be discussed?** — Health Team Discussion Brief
5. **What happens next?** — Next-Action Record

## Integrate throughout

- the people you love;
- family history and inherited relevance;
- shared lifestyle and environmental risks;
- a loved one’s independent concerns;
- support and care roles;
- privacy and separate ownership of health decisions.

## Do not build as separate launch workbooks

- science lessons;
- test explainers;
- generic question lists;
- generic habit plans;
- a separate prevention module;
- duplicate summary forms.

---

# 11. Matters to validate before final production

- whether users understand “heart risk audit” without further explanation;
- whether one workbook or a guided web version is easier;
- whether users prefer “What do we know?” or “Bring the facts together” as the component heading;
- whether the People You Love follow-up note should be a separate detachable page;
- whether a reusable copy may be used to help another adult and how the licence and privacy wording should state this;
- whether the first-pass completion target is realistic;
- whether the output pages are genuinely usable in short appointments;
- whether users can distinguish inherited, shared, separate, and support needs without learning those labels.
