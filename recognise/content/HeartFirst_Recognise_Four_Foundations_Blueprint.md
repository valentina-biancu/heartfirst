## The organising principle

The four foundations answer four different questions:

1. **What are we trying to protect?**
2. **What can place the cardiovascular system under strain from within?**
3. **What in our lives and surroundings can shape cardiovascular strain?**
4. **What does the standard cholesterol test show, and what remains outside it?**

Together they create the progression:

> **The system → internal risk → lived exposure → familiar baseline → Clarify what is known and unknown**

The questions should be visible to readers on the Recognise hub and on each resource page.

# Foundation 1

## The Heart We Are Trying to Protect

**Kicker:** RECOGNISE · THE HEART  
**Public question:** **What are we trying to protect?**  
**Working slug:** `/recognise/the-heart/`

### Reader promise

Understand how the heart, circulation, and blood work together, and why cardiovascular risk is rarely one isolated problem.

### Primary job

Make the cardiovascular system concrete before discussing risk.

### Essential content

- the heart as a muscular pump;
- four chambers and valves;
- the heart’s own coronary blood supply;
- electrical timing and rhythm;
- arteries, veins, pressure, and vascular resistance;
- blood transport, particles, clotting, and immune signals;
- flow, oxygen delivery, load, and reserve;
- distinct cardiovascular failure pathways;
- why one reassuring result cannot represent every dimension.

### Six friends

- **What?** The heart and wider cardiovascular system.
- **Where?** Heart muscle, valves, electrical system, coronary arteries, wider vessels, blood, and connected organs.
- **When?** Continuously, with some problems developing gradually and others appearing suddenly.
- **Why?** Every organ depends on adequate circulation.
- **Who?** Everyone, with individual risk shaped by history, health, exposures, inherited factors, and disease.
- **How?** Mechanical contraction, electrical coordination, valves, vessels, blood flow, and adaptation to demand.

### Key distinction

The heart is more than a pump, and cardiovascular risk is more than coronary cholesterol.

### What this does not mean

- Every cardiovascular risk interacts in the same way.
- Feeling well proves all parts of the system are healthy.
- One normal or abnormal result explains the whole system.
- The guide can assess personal risk.

### Natural hand-off

**The Risks That Build Within the Body**

### Source assets

- `MC1_The Heart We’re Trying to Protect.pdf`
- existing rebuilt content master;
- authoritative anatomy, physiology, rhythm, valve, and atherosclerosis sources.

### Derived assets

- At a Glance page;
- Four Ways to See the Heart and Circulation;
- Heart Risk Is Often a Time-and-Load Story;
- word-light visual plus accessible text alternative;
- PDF, print, email, social, and audio versions.

# Foundation 2

## The Risks That Build Within the Body

**Kicker:** RECOGNISE · INTERNAL AND CARDIOMETABOLIC RISK  
**Public question:** **What can place the cardiovascular system under strain from within?**  
**Working slug:** `/recognise/internal-risk/`

### Reader promise

Recognise the internal processes, measurements, inherited factors, and established findings that can influence cardiovascular risk, while understanding that they do not all mean the same thing.

### Primary job

Give the reader a broad but disciplined map of internal risk.

### Essential content domains

#### Pressure and vascular load

- blood pressure;
- vascular resistance;
- long-term pressure exposure;
- kidney and brain consequences.

#### Glucose regulation and metabolic context

- insulin resistance;
- prediabetes and diabetes;
- triglycerides and metabolic patterns;
- central adiposity as context, without reducing risk to body size.

#### Lipid and particle-related risk

- LDL cholesterol and non-HDL cholesterol;
- ApoB and particle number;
- Lipoprotein(a) (Lp(a));
- inherited lipid disorders;
- cumulative exposure.

#### Kidney context

- kidney function and cardiovascular risk;
- the two-way relationship between cardiovascular and kidney disease.

#### Plaque and established disease

- atherosclerosis;
- previous heart attack, stroke, peripheral arterial disease;
- imaging and subclinical plaque as separate information.

#### Structure, valves, rhythm, and heart muscle

- structural findings;
- valve disease;
- arrhythmias;
- heart-muscle disease;
- heart failure as a clinical syndrome, not a single risk factor.

#### Inflammation and clotting

- explain separately;
- distinguish general inflammatory biology, measured markers, clotting tendency, and specific clinical disorders;
- avoid a generic “inflammation score”.

#### Inherited and family context

- family history;
- inherited conditions;
- what family history can suggest and what it cannot establish.

### Six friends

- **What?** Internal risk factors, disease processes, measurements, and established findings.
- **Where?** Blood, arteries, heart, kidneys, metabolism, electrical system, valves, and inherited biology.
- **When?** Across the life course, sometimes quietly and sometimes after an event or diagnosis.
- **Why?** They can influence strain, plaque, blood flow, rhythm, reserve, and the likelihood or impact of disease.
- **Who?** Different people according to age, history, health, family, and established conditions.
- **How?** Through pressure, metabolic regulation, particles, plaque, organ function, rhythm, structure, and other pathways.

### Key distinction

Risk factors, biomarkers, diagnoses, symptoms, imaging findings, and established disease are not interchangeable.

### What this does not mean

- Everyone needs every test.
- Every factor compounds every other factor.
- A list of risk factors is a personal risk score.
- Cardiometabolic risk includes every possible cardiovascular problem.

### Natural hand-off

**The Risks We Live With**

### Source assets

# Foundation 3

## The Risks We Live With

**Kicker:** RECOGNISE · LIFESTYLE AND ENVIRONMENT  
**Public question:** **What in our lives and surroundings can shape cardiovascular strain?**  
**Working slug:** `/recognise/lifestyle-environment/`

### Reader promise

Understand how daily patterns, exposures, work, environment, and social conditions may influence cardiovascular health without turning risk into a judgement about character.

### Primary job

Restore the lifestyle and environmental leg of Recognise while preserving fairness, context, and agency.

### Essential content domains

#### Tobacco and nicotine

- smoking;
- vaping and nicotine;
- second-hand exposure;
- cessation as a health issue, not a moral judgement.

#### Food patterns

- overall dietary patterns;
- food quality and access;
- sodium, fibre, fats, and ultra-processed foods where evidence supports discussion;
- avoid one-food or purity narratives.

#### Movement and physical capacity

- physical activity;
- prolonged inactivity;
- cardiorespiratory fitness;
- disability, illness, work, safety, and access constraints.

#### Sleep

- quantity, quality, regularity;
- shift work;
- sleep-disordered breathing as a health-team issue.

#### Alcohol and other exposures

- amount and pattern;
- avoid claiming alcohol is required for heart health.

#### Stress and psychosocial strain

- chronic stress;
- isolation and support;
- caregiving;
- financial and work strain;
- trauma and mental-health context without overmedicalising ordinary stress.

#### Air, heat, noise, and work

- air pollution;
- extreme heat;
- chronic noise;
- selected occupational exposures;
- evidence and limits.

#### Social and environmental conditions

- housing;
- neighbourhood;
- access to care;
- food access;
- safe places to move;
- transport;
- income and work conditions.

### Six friends

- **What?** Lifestyle patterns and environmental, occupational, and social exposures.
- **Where?** Home, work, transport, neighbourhood, food environment, and wider society.
- **When?** Across daily life and over the life course, with acute and cumulative effects.
- **Why?** They can influence pressure, metabolism, sleep, inflammation, behaviour, access, and recovery.
- **Who?** Everyone, but exposure and control are distributed unequally.
- **How?** Through biological pathways, repeated exposure, available choices, resources, and barriers.

### Key distinction

**Modifiable** does not mean **easy to modify**, fully chosen, or the person’s fault.

### What this does not mean

- Lifestyle is the only cause of cardiovascular disease.
- People can remove every risk through willpower.
- Environmental risks affect everyone equally.
- One diet, exercise plan, or supplement is appropriate for everyone.

### Natural hand-off

**Your Cholesterol Test Is Useful. But It Does Not Show Every Heart Risk.**

### Source assets

- lifestyle and exposure sections in `MC2_We Live Inside a Cardiovascular Epidemic.pdf`;
- relevant book chapters and articles;
- new evidence audit across public-health, environmental, occupational, sleep, diet, movement, tobacco, and social-determinant sources.

### Derived assets

- lifestyle and environment At a Glance;
- “modifiable is not always easily modifiable” concept card;
- exposure checklist for discussion, not scoring;
- web, PDF, print, social, email, and audio versions.

# Foundation 4

## Your Cholesterol Test Is Useful. But It Does Not Show Every Heart Risk.

**Kicker:** RECOGNISE · CHOLESTEROL TESTING  
**Public question:** **What does the standard cholesterol test show, and what remains outside it?**  
**Working slug:** `/recognise/cholesterol-test/`

### Reader promise

Understand why the standard lipid panel remains essential, what it usually measures, and why a “normal” result does not establish that every relevant cardiovascular risk has been assessed.

### Primary job

Create the clean, evidence-led bridge from Recognise into Clarify.

### Essential content

#### Respect the test

- one of prevention’s major successes;
- familiar, scalable, actionable;
- tied to proven treatment and monitoring decisions;
- not obsolete because it is incomplete.

#### Explain what it usually includes

- total cholesterol;
- HDL cholesterol;
- triglycerides;
- calculated or measured LDL cholesterol;
- often non-HDL cholesterol;
- variation by laboratory, country, and context.

#### Explain what it can help with

- baseline lipid information;
- risk estimation in context;
- dyslipidaemia recognition;
- treatment decisions and follow-up;
- identifying very high values requiring clinical attention.

#### Explain lipid information outside the usual panel

- Lipoprotein(a) (Lp(a));
- ApoB unless separately ordered;
- discordance and specialised particle information.

#### Explain wider information outside the panel’s purpose

- blood pressure;
- glucose regulation;
- kidney function;
- smoking and nicotine;
- family history;
- symptoms;
- rhythm;
- valves and structure;
- plaque imaging;
- lifestyle and environmental context;
- established diagnoses;
- selected inflammatory and clotting information.

#### Explain false reassurance carefully

A result may be reassuring for the question it answered while remaining incomplete for a wider question.

#### Preserve global context

In settings where basic lipid testing is unavailable, access to a standard panel may itself be the priority.

### Six friends

- **What?** A laboratory assessment of selected blood lipids.
- **Where?** Blood, with implications interpreted in the wider cardiovascular context.
- **When?** At baseline, during risk assessment, when diagnosing lipid disorders, and when monitoring treatment or change.
- **Why?** It provides actionable information connected to strong prevention evidence.
- **Who?** Use depends on age, risk, clinical context, health system, and professional guidance.
- **How?** Through measured and calculated lipid values interpreted alongside other relevant information.

### Key distinction

A standard lipid panel is not a complete cardiovascular risk assessment by design.

It provides important lipid information, but it does not establish whether other relevant risks have been measured, recorded, connected, or discussed.

### What this does not mean

- The panel is poor, obsolete, or dispensable.
- Everyone needs advanced testing.
- Every risk outside the panel is hidden.
- A normal result is meaningless.
- The resource can determine which additional tests a person needs.

### Clarify bridge

Close with:

> You may now have several pieces of information: what the cardiovascular system needs, the risks that may develop within the body, the risks shaped by lifestyle and environment, and what a standard cholesterol test does and does not show.
>
> The next question is:
>
> **What is already known, and what remains unknown or incomplete?**
>
> Clarify heart risk helps organise the information you already have, identify what may still be missing or unclear, prepare what is worth discussing, and record the next action agreed with your health team.
